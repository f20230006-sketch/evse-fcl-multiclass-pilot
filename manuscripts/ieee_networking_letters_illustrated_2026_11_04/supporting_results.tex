\section*{Scope and interpretation}
The main letter focuses on the allocation comparison. This supplement retains the complete main table, exact-type sensitivity, memory-300 comparison and all federated/centralized split comparisons. No new training or selection was performed for this revision.

RBR has lower Slowloris recall than protected replay at memory 60 and 300. At memory 300, protected also exceeds RBR on old accuracy. RBR's federated exact-type old-accuracy and Slowloris differences against CBRS are not significant; centralized order 2 favors CBRS on Slowloris. Per-order results and intervals remain in the accompanying CSVs.

\section*{Validation boundaries}
Data come from one dataset and constructed clients. Role splits overlap and are not independent captures. Source filenames provide file groups, but no explicit session/capture column was found; only two benign and two Slowloris files prevent all three roles from having whole-file support. Sixteen benign and five fragmentation evaluation flows preclude reliable false-alarm and fragmentation claims. Task-1 categorical vocabularies differ by order, mixing representation and sequence effects. Intervals use ten paired training-seed averages across three orders and are unadjusted for multiple comparisons. Non-significance does not establish non-inferiority.

The CBRS population is evaluated under the shared task-boundary protocol and uniform replay retrieval, not the original paper's separate weighted-replay training recipe. Its federated retention/Slowloris significance depends on merged scoring. The rare+random ablation also varies calibrated quotas and within-group sampling, limiting single-component causal attribution. EWC was calibrated over a limited lambda range. A faithful tabular FedCBDR baseline is outside scope. Replay capacity counts rows, not total RAM; deployment, attack blocking and formal privacy guarantees were not evaluated.
